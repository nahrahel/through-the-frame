How it was made

A rendered film, not a slideshow

Picture

The film was rendered frame by frame at 1280 × 720 and 24 frames per second from the six images you supplied. A virtual camera glides, pushes in and pulls back across each painting, and each work gets its own motion. Van Gogh's sky turns and his stars pulse. Hokusai's sea heaves, Munch's sunset ripples, Zephyr's roses drift and Vermeer's pearl catches the light.

Sound

The score was synthesized note by note for this film from soft pads, plucked strings and bell tones in a hall reverb. Each painting has its own key and texture: a lute for Botticelli, glass bells for Vermeer, koto-like runs and surf for Hokusai, swirling arpeggios for Van Gogh and a rising dissonance for Munch. A chime marks every caption and a low bloom marks every reveal.

The walk

The rooms on this page follow the same camera paths as the film, but your scrolling drives them. The light, petals, stars, spray and heat haze in each room are drawn live in your browser on top of the real painting.

Limits worth knowing. Nothing in the paintings was repainted or generated by AI. All the motion comes from moving and gently warping the real images. Only the six paintings supplied for this project appear in the film; the other works in the collection below link out to their full images. The page streams the film as eight chapter files that play in sync with one continuous score, so it starts quickly. The Scream image used here is 500 pixels wide, so its close-ups are softer than the others. Download the whole film (MP4, 4:26).
